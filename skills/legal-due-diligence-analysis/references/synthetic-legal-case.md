# Synthetic Legal Due-Diligence Case

Use only for testing the skill's reasoning. It is not a real company or legal advice.

## Project context

Example Materials Co. operates a small industrial site, has 68 employees and uses several contractors. Its core value depends on a process patent family and long-term customer contracts. The investment is a preferred-equity transaction in one jurisdiction, but the entity perimeter has not yet been confirmed.

## Supplied materials

- Current cap table says Founder A owns 60%, Founder B 25%, and an employee pool 15%; an older financing summary lists a different pool and an unrecorded pledge.
- The patent register lists the company as applicant, but the inventor agreement and employee assignment for two inventors are missing. One patent is shown as granted in a management spreadsheet, while a public record supplied by the user shows an application still pending in one jurisdiction.
- A customer contract has a change-of-control consent clause and an exclusivity clause. Only a PDF summary is supplied; the signed contract and amendments are missing.
- HR provides a headcount list and standard employment template. It does not provide social-insurance reconciliation, contractor agreements, safety-training records, or the last three years of accident/work-injury and labor-dispute records.
- Management says “no material disputes or work injuries”. An internal email mentions one contractor injury and an unresolved compensation discussion; no claim file or insurance notification is supplied.
- A supplier has sent a demand letter for unpaid invoices. Management says the amount is disputed, but no contract, delivery records, or correspondence file is supplied.

## Expected behavior

The skill should route corporate/ownership, registration history, governance, contracts, IP, labor/work-injury, disputes, financing/security, and EHS workstreams; preserve the cap-table and patent-status conflicts; refuse to treat “no material disputes” as clearance; request the missing injury, insurance, contract, assignment, court/claim, and ownership evidence; distinguish public status checks from legal conclusions; map each material issue to investability, valuation, structure/closing, and investor-protection implications; and route enforceability, liability, and transaction-condition judgments to counsel.
